# Kata — explain it to a twelve-year-old

Runs `/simplify-this` against something you own. Proven form: the skill exists and has
been used in anger, so it qualifies under the two-runs rule.

The test of understanding is not whether you can use the jargon. It is whether you can
put it down.

## Form

1. Pick something you built or maintain and could explain in jargon without thinking.
2. Write the explanation for a smart person who has not seen this domain. Ten minutes.
3. Diagram before paragraph at three or more steps.
4. Re-read for the banned words — *obviously, simply, just, of course, trivially*. Each
   one is a place you skipped work and charged the reader for it.
5. Name the analogy you used, and where it breaks. Every analogy breaks; an unnamed
   breaking point is how a reader ends up confidently wrong.

## Subject selection

Prefer what you understand *least well among things you own*. The comfortable explanation
teaches nothing.

## Record

- The analogy, and its breaking point.
- Any jargon you could not remove, and what it would cost to remove it.

## Signal to watch

Banned-word count per rep, trending down. And whether the breaking point gets easier to
find — noticing where your own analogy fails is the actual skill here.
